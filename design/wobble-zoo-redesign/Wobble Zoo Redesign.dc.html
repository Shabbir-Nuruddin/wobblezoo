1Full redesign — brief, name, and five screens
The read

The game is good. The screens are apologising for it.

Three things are wrong today, and they are all the same thing. Everything is a stack of pills. Play, Levels, Settings, Hint, Undo, Reset — same shape, same weight, no hierarchy, so nothing feels like the main event. Nothing has a place. The zoo, the picker and the settings are panels dropped over the menu; text collides with the logo and with itself. The zoo is a list, not a home — a horizontal scroll of portraits that you cannot touch.

So: one warm, lamplit world with rooms instead of panels. One primary action per screen, big and unmistakable. And a zoo that is a dorm you visit — animals awake, wandering, hungry, tuckable.

The name

"Wobble" describes a physics game you deleted two pivots ago. Three that describe this game:

Tuck InThe verb of the game. Two syllables, warm, searchable-ish.
MoonpileOdd, memorable, ownable. Reads as animals in a heap.
Sleepy ShuffleSays the mechanic out loud. Safest of the three.

Screens below use Tuck In. It's a one-line swap if you'd rather keep Wobble Zoo.

System
One primary per screen. Terracotta, chunky, with a pressable bottom edge. Nothing else gets that treatment.
Secondary = outline pill. Tertiary = round icon on a ghost disc.
Cream is day, deep umber is night. Home and zoo are lamplit warm; the puzzle rooms stay night.
Caprasimo for numbers, names, level titles. Figtree for everything you read.
Stars are gold, snacks are amber. One is progress, one is affection. They never mix.
